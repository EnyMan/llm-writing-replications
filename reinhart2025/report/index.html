Do LLMs write like humans? A replication, and 25 models later

We reran Reinhart et al. (2025) on 900 human texts from the HAP-E corpus and reproduced the paper's effect sizes (r = — over — feature × model pairs). Then we gave the same prompt to — current models: OpenRouter's most used, Claude through Claude Code, and community finetunes that promise more human prose. All of them are still easy to tell from human writing (a classifier separates even the closest one —% of the time), but the average stylistic gap is smaller than GPT-4o's: mean |d| of — for —, against — for GPT-4o.

Each dot is one Biber feature for one of the paper's six models: the paired Cohen's d the paper reports (Table S6) against the one we measured on our 900-document subset with pybiber. Dots on the diagonal agree exactly.

Cohen's d is the mean paired difference (model − human continuation of the same text) divided by its standard deviation. 0.2 is small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large.

Method and caveats
  • Texts: the 900 documents of HAP-E mini (150 each of academic, blog, fiction, news, spoken, TV/movie). Each model got human chunk 1 and the paper's prompt: "In the same style, tone, and diction of the following text, complete the next 500 words, generate exactly 500 words, and note that the text does not necessarily end after the generated words:". It is compared with the real chunk 2.
  • Features: Biber's 67 features from pybiber 0.3.1 with spaCy en_core_web_sm, set to match the paper's pseudobibeR output (plain type-token ratio, lenient "be" as main verb). The paper's six models were re-tagged with the same pipeline rather than taken from the published values.
  • Filtering as in the paper: responses under 100 words are dropped, as are refusals and safety-filter errors. GPT-6 Luna refused or cut short 23% of prompts, mostly TV scripts and news it judged copyrighted; Claude Haiku refused 6-7% and Opus 2-4%; most other models lost under 1%.
  • A last paragraph that only talks about the prompt ("*Note: this continuation is exactly 500 words…*") is removed before tagging, for every model. Claude Haiku through Claude Code added one to 79% of its outputs.
  • Providers: OpenRouter for most models, NanoGPT for the uncensored, Gembrain and Antislop finetunes and their Qwen 3.8 27B parent, Ollama on a local RTX 4090 for Hemmingway-1 (Q4_K_M), and Claude Code (claude -p) on a subscription for Haiku, Sonnet and Opus 5.5. Claude ran at low effort; Sonnet and Opus barely think, Haiku still thinks about 5,000 tokens per text.
  • Classifiers: 75/25 train/test split by document, stratified by genre; random forest with 500 trees; lasso logistic regression with 10-fold CV.
  • Reasoning was turned off where a model allows it. MiniMax and ERNIE reason regardless and, told to write "exactly 500 words", counted words until they ran out of tokens; they got one extra sentence ("Do not count the words; an approximate length of about 500 words is fine."). A paired test on two other models found no feature that this sentence changes (0 of 67 after Bonferroni).
  • Not lemmatized: vocabulary counts use lowercased word forms, and curly and straight apostrophes are treated as the same character. Many 2026 models write typographic apostrophes (it’s) where the human texts use straight ones.
  • API generation cost about $15.80 across 27 models on OpenRouter and NanoGPT, pilots included. Claude through Claude Code ran on a subscription, and Hemmingway-1 ran locally.
